# Jon's Thinking: Strategy & Positioning

This is how Jon frames SuperBase in [[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]], and what it means for the promo video. The notes read *between* the lines of the document. Where something is my inference rather than Jon's words, it's marked **(inference)**.

## 1. The one-sentence idea

> **Your yard's existing base can become the hardstand, so stop paying to patch it and stop pricing concrete you can't justify.**

The cover headline carries it: **"Stop patching the yard. Bind it."** The promise is the two-word verb *Bind it*. Everything else in the document is supporting evidence.

## 2. The market frame: a false binary, then a third option

Jon sets up the buyer's current choice as a dilemma and then breaks it:

| Option | What the buyer lives with | Jon's framing |
|---|---|---|
| **A. "Keep making it good"** (grader + patching) | A recurring opex treadmill: potholes, ruts, loose stone, mud, dust, downtime | *"The yard never actually gets finished."* |
| **B. "Pour a slab"** (engineered concrete) | High capex, weeks out of service, fixed layout, point-load cracking, minimum quantities | *"Why concrete stalls."* |
| **C. SuperBase** | Binds the base already in the yard, area by area, trafficable within hours, 60–70% below concrete | *"There is a third option."* |

This is a classic **"third way" positioning** (inference). SuperBase isn't pitched against competing binders or stabilisers, which the document never mentions. It's pitched against the two things a yard manager is *already* doing or pricing. So the video should compete with the **grader and the concrete quote**, not with other chemicals.

## 3. Who Jon is talking to (inference from the content)

- **Primary buyer:** the operator or owner of an **industrial or plant yard**: plant hire, quarries and bulk materials, logistics and transport depots, laydown yards, mining-support hardstand, possibly council works depots. The document's vocabulary gives it away: *plant standing, stockpile aprons, laydown, trailer marshalling, load-out, gate approaches*.
- **Their pains, in their own words:** ruts, potholes, mud on the public road at the gate, dust where people work, tyres chewed by loose stone, the grader "coming back again".
- **What they have to justify:** a spend that competes with a concrete quote. So Jon gives them *finance-grade* material: a 60–70% rate differential, a trial report with CBR numbers, and a business case set "beside a concrete quote and a year of patching".
- **Secondary readers:** site engineers and approvers (method statements, dosage design, soaked CBR, density), plus the operators' own crews (training and handover).

## 4. The argument structure (why the document works)

1. **Hook:** name the pain and the promise (cover).
2. **Agitate:** the maintenance cycle *and* the reasons concrete stalls (p.2).
3. **Reframe:** "there is a third option" (p.2 call-out).
4. **Mechanism:** how it's built, in 5 plain steps with real site photos (pp.3–4).
5. **Proof:** "measured, not asserted": soaked CBR before and after, on *your* material (p.4).
6. **Fit:** one product dosed differently per yard area (p.5).
7. **Credibility:** in commercial use since 2017, Eloise Copper Project (p.6).
8. **Low-risk ask:** "Nominate one area." A trial, not a contract (p.6).
9. **Guard rails:** the disclaimer (p.6).

That's **Problem → Agitate → Solve → Prove → Ask**, a standard B2B persuasion arc. It also maps cleanly onto a 30–60 s promo (see §9).

## 5. Key ideas Jon keeps coming back to

| Theme | Where it appears | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Use what you already have** | Cover ("the yard base you already have"), p.3 ("usually the material already in the yard is all that is needed"), p.5 | Removes the biggest cost line (imported material) and the psychological barrier of a "new pavement" |
| **Built, not coated** | p.3 ("not a surface spray"), p.4 ("the surface is built, not coated") | Heads off the objection that it's just a dust-suppression spray that wears off. The binder is **through the full depth** |
| **Ordinary plant, no specialist crew** | Cover ("grader, water cart and roller, with no paving crew and no pour"), p.6 (crew training, handover) | Lower cost, and the customer can own the process. The footage shows exactly this kit |
| **Speed / no downtime** | p.4 ("trafficable within hours of the final roll"), p.6 ("staged so the yard keeps trading") | Direct counter to "the yard stops" under concrete |
| **Measured, not asserted** | p.4 (soaked CBR), p.6 ("rather than a claim") | Jon is deliberately *anti-hype*. The brand voice is engineering-credible, not salesy |
| **Area by area / modular** | p.2, p.5, p.6 | Beats concrete's minimum quantities and fixed layout, and makes the trial the natural first step |
| **Water is the enemy** | p.2 (water gets into the base), p.3 (crossfall stops ponding; "ponded water is where potholes start"), p.4 (tested *soaked*) | A clear, visual causal story: water → pothole. SuperBase + correct fall breaks it |

## 6. The commercial model (inference)

- **Land:** yard walk, then a **paid or subsidised trial area** (the document doesn't say which), then a CBR trial report.
- **Expand:** "roll out by area", staged, dosed by duty (heavy vs light).
- **Retain:** "handover" plus "crew training so your own operators run it". The recurring revenue is probably **binder supply and logistics** ("supply and logistics to your yard"), with Maintain as the dosage-design and testing authority.
- **Low-dose upsell:** gate approaches and perimeter get treated "by water cart as part of routine yard watering". That's an easy, ongoing consumable use.
- **Top-coat upsell:** an organic top coat over high-wear running lines.

## 7. How Jon handles risk and credibility

- The **disclaimer** on p.6 is thorough: figures are indicative; rates, dosage, depth, cure times, CBR and savings vary; SuperBase *is not a substitute for engineered ground assessment*; critical loads are assessed separately.
- Every big claim is **anchored to a test** (CBR, density) or **qualified** ("usually", "approximately 75 mm", "within hours").
- **Reference project:** the *Eloise Copper Project* (a whole-site application including truck and forklift hardstand) is used as *analogous duty*, and Jon is careful to say "your base material and traffic are different again".
- **Implication for the video:** we can't make harder claims than the document does. See [[Claims Register]] for video-safe wording. Any on-screen "60–70%" needs an "indicative / vs engineered concrete / confirmed by site trial" qualifier.

## 8. Tone and voice

- Plain, confident, **tradie-meets-engineer** Australian English: *yard*, *making good*, *bull dust*, *the grader comes back*, *keeps trading*.
- Short declarative sentences and rhythm-driven repetition ("So the grader comes back. And comes back again.").
- **Antithesis lines** that work as voiceover or supers:
  - "Stop patching the yard. Bind it."
  - "Compaction builds the bond. Testing proves it."
  - "The strength gain is measured, not asserted."
  - "Proven as hardstand. Tested on your yard."
  - "Built, not coated."
  - "Nominate one area."
- **No hype words.** No "revolutionary" or "game-changing". The video should keep that restraint.

## 10. Gaps and open questions for Jon

1. **Trial commercial terms:** is the trial area free, paid, or credited against roll-out?
2. **Eloise Copper Project:** may we name it on screen? Are there photos or footage we can use?
3. **Footage rights and location:** where were these clips shot (one site or several), and have the site owner and any identifiable workers consented to promo use?
4. **Can we state a typical CBR uplift?** The document deliberately doesn't give a number. Confirm we should keep it that way.
5. **Product form:** what does SuperBase look like (liquid concentrate, IBC totes, how it's dosed into the water cart)? A 1–2 s "product" shot would help the video.
6. **Top coat:** is the "organic top coat" a separate named product?
7. **Target channels:** LinkedIn, website hero, sales-meeting loop, trade shows? This decides 16:9 vs 9:16 vs 1:1 (the footage is **portrait 9:16**).
8. **Fonts:** the PDF embeds rasterised Type3 fonts, so we need the actual typeface names for supers.
9. **Voiceover:** Jon's own voice (authentic) vs an AI voice?
