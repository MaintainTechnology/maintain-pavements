# Promo Video Brief: Maintain Pavements SuperBase

Back to [[00 Home]] · Plan: [[Script & Storyboard]] · Clips: [[Shot Library]] · Build: [[CapCut Edit Guide]] / [[Remotion Build Notes]] · AI: [[AI Generation Prompts]]
Sources: [[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]] · [[Jon's Thinking - Strategy & Positioning]] · [[Claims Register]] · [[Brand & Visual Language]] · [[Footage Index]]

## 1. Objective

Get yard owners and operators to **nominate one area** for a SuperBase trial.

In 30 to 60 seconds the video has to do three things:

1. Name the pain the buyer already pays for: the yard that keeps going backwards, and the grader that keeps coming back.
2. Show the third option working, using Jon's real site footage in his five-step order: prepare, dose and mix, grade to level, roll and compact, cure and return to service.
3. Hand over the next step and Jon's number, with the numbers qualified exactly as the capability statement qualifies them.

A secondary job for the 60 s cut is a loop Jon can play in sales meetings, on the website and at trade shows that explains the method in under a minute.

## 2. Audience

From [[Jon's Thinking - Strategy & Positioning]] §3 (the audience itself is an inference from the document's vocabulary):

| Who | What they live with | What the video must show them |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary:** owners, operators and site managers of industrial and plant yards (plant hire, quarries and bulk materials, logistics and transport depots, laydown yards, mining-support hardstand, possibly council works depots) | Ruts, potholes, loose stone chewing tyres, mud tracked onto the road at the gate, dust where people work, the grader "coming back again", and a concrete quote they can't justify | It binds the base they already have, it's built with ordinary plant (grader, water cart, roller), it's back in service quickly, and the numbers get tested on their own yard |
| **Secondary:** site engineers and approvers | Need method and test evidence, not adjectives | "Soaked CBR tested, before and after, on your own yard material." No hype words |
| **Secondary:** the operators' crews | Will run it after handover | Plant they already know. "We train your crew. Your plant runs it." |

The video competes with **the grader and the concrete quote**, not with other binders. The document never mentions competing products and neither should the video.

## 3. Single-minded message

> **Stop patching the yard. Bind it.**

It's Jon's cover headline. It opens the reframe and closes the end card in every cut. The support line is Jon's p.2 call-out: *"There is a third option."*

## 5. Tone

- Plain, confident, tradie-meets-engineer Australian English: *yard*, *making good*, *the grader comes back*, *keeps trading*.
- Short declarative lines with Jon's rhythm ("So the grader comes back. And comes back again.").
- Show the work. The real machines and the real surface carry the persuasion; the graphics stay charcoal, grey and white and never shout.
- No hype words ("revolutionary", "game-changing"), no stock-footage gloss, no fake testimonials.
- Safety-conscious: don't glamorise people on foot beside moving plant (several clips have this; see [[Shot Library]]).

## 9. Music and sound

- **Music:** a driving, low-key instrumental bed at **120 BPM**, where one beat is 0.5 s, so every cut in the storyboard (all on 0.5 s boundaries) lands on a beat. Muted kick, tight low synth-bass pulse, a little metallic percussion, no vocals. It builds in two steps (method start, value panel) and ends on one low hit with a short ring-out under the end card. It needs a commercial licence (stock library, or an AI music tool whose terms allow commercial use). A prompt is in [[AI Generation Prompts]].
- **Natural sound:** keep the real machine sound from each clip at a low level under the music (roughly -28 to -24 dB below the mix peak). It gives the footage weight. There is **no speech in any clip**: faster-whisper with VAD found zero speech segments, and the non-VAD "Thank you for watching" text is a known hallucination on noise.
- **Hits:** V02B has a strong low-frequency onset at about 0.7–1.0 s in the source (it lands on the roll beat in every cut). V03B peaks at 6.5 s, just after the closest pass. Use them as the "drum" moments.
- **Duck:** the pulsed alarm-like signals in V03A, V03B and V03C (consistent with reversing alarms, inference) fight a 120 BPM track. Duck them under the VO and music.
- **AI shots:** mute the generated audio and use licensed library SFX (rain, wind, grader blade).

## 11. What the footage can and can't carry

These limits come straight from the verified footage notes. They shape the script.

- **Step 03 Grade to level has no footage.** No clip shows a grader. The storyboard uses Jon's own p.5 photo of a grader (`PDF-p5_img1.jpeg`, a different site) with a crossfall line graphic. The fix is a 5–8 s pickup of the grader trimming at the next job. Jon also used a V03C roller still as his step 03 photo, but that shows a roller, not grading.
- **Dose and mix shows water going on, not mixing through the depth.** V02A shows a water cart and hand hose. Whether the liquid carried binder can't be seen. Jon's PDF captions the V02A frame "Binder and water through the full depth", so the script uses his step label and has the "full depth" message carried by the VO and an illustrative diagram, not by a caption on the spray.
- **Only V02B shows water at rolling** (a hand hose ahead of the drum). The low-sun roller clips (V03A, V03B, V03C) show no water, so they never carry "wet rolling" as a caption.
- **The only end-state images are two stills.** S04B shows the finished-looking pad under the same canopy; its treatment and cure timing are unconfirmed. S04A is Jon's p.5 "Finished bound surface" photo from a different, unidentified yard. Neither proves "within hours" or any strength figure.
- **V04 is a duplicate of V03C** (identical audio, same 552 frames) and is misfiled under cure. It is rolling, not cure. Edit from V03C, the higher-bitrate copy, and never use both.
- **All clips are WhatsApp transcodes**, 576 px wide at roughly 0.9 to 1.6 Mbps. Expect softness at 1080 px; keep all text as crisp overlays.

## 13. Open decisions (for Jon)

1. **Channels.** Which platforms first? This decides the priority order of 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9 (Jon's Thinking, open question 7).
2. **Voice.** Jon's own voice, an AI voice, or both (an AI draft, then Jon records the final)?
3. **Logo and fonts.** Vector logo files and the real typeface names (the PDF fonts are rasterised Type3).
4. **Footage rights and consent.** Who filmed the WhatsApp clips, and may they be used commercially? Consent from the operators and ground crew (faces are partly visible in V01, V02A and V02B), and from the site owner.
5. **Third-party marks.** CAT logos appear on almost every machine. Decide whether to show or soften them. The Hastings Deering dealer decal (V03C 9.0–10.5 s) and the fleet numbers "740" and "5335" should be blurred unless the owners agree.
6. **Liquid on screen.** Is the V02A cart and hose applying SuperBase with water, or plain water? What's in the IBC totes (V02A 0.0–2.0 s)? The answer decides whether a product beat is possible.
7. **Step 03 pickup.** Can someone film 5–8 s of the grader trimming to level and crossfall, portrait, at the next job? It would replace the stand-in still.
8. **End-state.** Is S04B the treated pad from the V01–V04 job, and how long after the final roll was it taken? Is there a photo or clip of plant on the finished pad?
9. **Named reference.** May the Eloise Copper Project be named, and can "in commercial use since 2017" be used?
10. **AI budget and approval.** Who approves each paid generation? The problem and diagram shots in [[AI Generation Prompts]] are the only ones proposed.
11. **Trial terms.** Is the trial area free, paid or credited? The CTA only says "Nominate one area", so it doesn't depend on this, but sales follow-up does.
