# Step 4: Roll and Compact

← [[Step 3 - Grade to Level]] · next → [[Step 5 - Cure and Return to Service]] · [[Process Overview]] · [[00 Home]]

> [!summary] In one line
> A smooth-drum roller compacts the treated base while it's still wet, and that compaction is what creates the bond and the strength. **This is the best-covered step: four unique clips (65.3 s) of CAT smooth-drum roller 5335 under the canopy, one of them with water sprayed ahead of the drum.** It's also where two misfiled clips really belong (V02B and V04).

## What Jon says

> Wet rolling to final compaction with a smooth drum roller. This is the stage that develops the bond — the surface is built, not coated — and it is where the surface strength comes from. Compaction is confirmed on site against the density nominated in the dosage design. ([[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]], p.4)

Page headline: *"Compaction builds the bond. Testing proves it."* Photo caption: *"04 — Wet rolling to final compaction"*, a frame from V02B at about 0.5 s.

**Applications (p.5):** heavy plant standing areas get "binder and water applied across all processes through to final wet roll". The outcomes rest on this step: "holds its shape under concentrated loads", aprons that don't push out and rut, standing that doesn't bed in.

**Claims** ([[Claims Register]]): **C8** "Compaction builds the bond." (strength comes from compaction; density confirmed against the dosage design); **C6** "Built, not coated."; **C5** the roller; **C10** holds its shape.

## Why it matters

- **Compaction is where the strength comes from** (Jon, C8). Rolling squeezes the treated material into a dense layer with the particles pressed together, so the binder holds them in contact. A loosely packed layer keeps voids that let water in and let stones move under load (inference, general pavement logic).
- **Wet rolling** compacts while the treated base still holds its moisture (the glossary: "compacting while the treated base is still moist"). V02B shows the crew keeping the strip wet just ahead of the drum.
- **Built, not coated.** The bond is made through the layer as it's compacted, not painted on top. That's the answer to "isn't it just a dust spray?" (C6).
- **It's measured.** Compaction is checked on site against the density set in the dosage design, and it feeds the trial report ("depth and compaction", p.4). That's the first half of the proof loop; see [[Process Overview]].
- **Ordinary plant.** A standard smooth-drum roller, which the customer's crew can run after training (C5, C15).
