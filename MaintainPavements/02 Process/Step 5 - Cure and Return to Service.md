## Why it matters

- **This is the commercial heart of the pitch.** Concrete "takes the area out of service for weeks" (p.2); SuperBase is "trafficable within hours of the final roll". That's the difference between shutting a yard and staging it area by area so it "keeps trading" (C2, C16). The disclaimer still applies: cure times vary with material, climate and duty.
- **Cure turns a compacted layer into a bound one.** Rolled and wet, the surface isn't yet the finished product (V02B); once cured, it's the hardstand that holds its shape under loads (C10).
- **The top coat is for wear, not structure.** Running lines take turning, scrubbing tyres (the kind of marks S04A shows, inference), so they can get a sprayed organic dressing over the compacted base. The strength is still in the layer underneath.
- **After cure is when the proof is taken.** The after-cure soaked CBR sits against the before-treatment figure in the trial report (C9). See the loop in [[Process Overview]].
