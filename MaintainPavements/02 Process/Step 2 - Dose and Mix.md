# Step 2: Dose and Mix

← [[Step 1 - Prepare]] · next → [[Step 3 - Grade to Level]] · [[Process Overview]] · [[00 Home]]

> [!summary] In one line
> SuperBase goes into the base with the water, at a rate designed for that yard's material, and is mixed through the whole treated layer. **Footage: the dose half is well shot (water cart and hose in V02A). The mix half is not shown at all, and nothing visible proves the liquid is SuperBase rather than water.**

## What Jon says

> SuperBase is introduced with water at a binder-to-water ratio set against your own base material, and mixed through the full depth of the layer. Where new base has been added, the binder runs through all moisture processes so the new layer does not delaminate from the old. ([[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]], p.3)

And the principle it rests on (p.3):

> SuperBase is not a pavement, and its primary mode is not a surface spray. It is an additive introduced into the base during the lay phase and carried through every moisture process, so the binder sits through the full depth of the layer rather than on top of it.

Photo caption: *"02 — Binder and water through the full depth"*, a frame from V02A at about 24.0–24.5 s.

## Why it matters

- **Into the layer, not onto it.** A surface spray leaves a thin treated film on top of loose material; traffic wears through it and water gets under it (inference, general pavement logic). Mixed through the full depth, the binder holds the whole ~75 mm layer together, so there's no skin to wear off. That's the difference between "built" and "coated" (C6).
- **Dosed to the material.** Every yard's base is different, so the binder-to-water ratio is set in the dosage design for *that* material, and the rate is varied by what each area carries (p.5). That's also why the trial report quotes the dosage rate that produced the result (p.4).
- **Carried through every moisture process.** Water goes on more than once (mixing, grading, rolling). Keeping the binder in every pass means any new roadbase added on top bonds to the old base instead of peeling off (C7).
- **The water cart is ordinary plant.** The customer already owns or hires one, which is part of why there's "no paving crew and no pour" (C5), and why the light dose on gate approaches can go on "as part of routine yard watering" (C12).

- **The mix.** No blade, ripper or other pass turning the binder through the depth. **This is the most important gap after Step 3**, because without it V02A reads as the surface spray Jon says SuperBase isn't.
