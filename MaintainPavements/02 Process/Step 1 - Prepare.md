# Step 1: Prepare

← [[Process Overview]] · next → [[Step 2 - Dose and Mix]] · [[00 Home]]

> [!summary] In one line
> Get the existing yard base ready to take the binder: strip the loose stuff off the top, loosen the base to about 75 mm, and only bring in new roadbase where the levels need it. **Footage: one clip (V01), which shows the loose, scarified-looking base being worked, but not the scarifying or grading-off itself.**

## What Jon says

> Loose stone and bull dust are graded off and the existing yard base is scarified to approximately 75 mm. Additional roadbase is imported only where design levels require it — usually the material already in the yard is all that is needed. ([[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]], p.3)

Photo caption: *"01 — Trimming the scarified base"*, a frame from V01 at 2.5–3.0 s.

**Where it shows up in the applications (p.5):** heavy plant standing areas are "re-levelled and scarified through the existing base". Every other area type starts from the same existing base, dosed to suit its duty.

**Claims** ([[Claims Register]]):
- **C3** scarified to "approximately 75 mm". Video-safe: "Scarified to around 75 mm."
- **C4** "usually the material already in the yard is all that is needed". Video-safe: "Uses the base you already have." (never "always")
- **C5** built with a grader, water cart and roller. The grader is the Prepare and Grade machine.
- **C16** staged area by area. Prepare is done one area at a time.

## Why it matters

- **Loose stone and bull dust can't be bound in place.** They're the loose material that ravels, chews tyres and turns to dust in the dry and mud in the wet (Jon's p.2 problem list). Leaving them on top would leave a weak, loose skin over the treated layer (inference).
- **Scarifying opens the base up so the binder can go *into* it.** SuperBase is "an additive introduced into the base … so the binder sits through the full depth of the layer rather than on top of it" (p.3). A compacted, unscarified surface would only take the binder at the top, which is exactly the "surface spray" Jon says it isn't. The ~75 mm is the depth of the layer that gets treated (C3).
- **Using the yard's own material is the cost story.** No imported pavement, no paving crew, no pour (C1, C4, C5). Roadbase is only brought in where design levels need it.
