# V02B: Wet rolling under the shed, CAT roller 5335 with hose crew

> [!summary] What this video is about
> A yellow CAT smooth-drum roller (fleet number **5335**) runs drum-first along a strip of a dark aggregate pad, beside an already-smooth edge lane, inside a large open-sided steel shed. A crew member in orange hi-vis walks ahead of the drum spraying a clear liquid (water, possibly binder-dosed; the pixels can't tell) from a hand-held hose, and a second crew member stands out on the hose line (apparently tending it, inference). The camera tracks side-on, swings round to a wide view down the shed, then walks in behind the roller and down to the freshly rolled, wet surface. Although it's filed under *02-dose-and-mix*, the pixels show **wet rolling (Jon's step 04)**, and Jon's own PDF uses a frame from this clip, captioned "04 — Wet rolling to final compaction". For the promo it's one of the strongest "built, not coated" sequences: clean machine, visible crew, water on the base, and tactile close-ups of the rolled surface.

## Deep analysis

### Process: what this shows about SuperBase

**Step shown: doc step 04, Roll and compact** → [[Step 4 - Roll and Compact]]. Jon's photo caption for step 04 in [[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]] is *"04 — Wet rolling to final compaction"*, and **that photo (PDF p4_img1) is a frame from this very clip, around 0.5 s**. Jon describes this stage as the one where *"the surface is built, not coated"*. The hose spraying ahead of the drum is the "wet" in wet rolling.

**Why it's filed under dose-and-mix.** Crew A is applying liquid to the base, which ties it to [[Step 2 - Dose and Mix]]. Jon writes that SuperBase *"goes in with water"* and that the binder *"runs through all moisture processes"*, and on p.5 that heavy plant areas get *"binder and water applied across all processes through to final wet roll"*. So the hose may well be carrying dosed water (inference), but **the pixels can't tell plain water from binder-and-water**. What's visible is a smooth-drum roller compacting a wetted base, so the note is tagged step 04 and does not match its folder. No grading or trimming is visible, so this clip gives **no evidence for [[Step 3 - Grade to Level]]**.

**Surface state, by zone**
- **Untreated or unrolled pad (foreground, 0–12 s):** dark grey to near-black crushed aggregate with individual stones visible and a dry-to-damp, open-looking texture. It reads as loose or not yet final-rolled (inference).
- **Lane being rolled (the strip next to the edge lane):** a band of wet sheen ahead of and under the drum (0–6 s), where crew A sprays. Behind the roller this lane carries the rear tyres' diamond tread prints over a wet film (13–22 s); the drum's own finish can't be seen because the tyres run over it.
- **Edge lane (between the roller's lane and the column footings):** smooth and reflective with a thin rippled water film (16–22 s). It was **already smooth and lighter grey before this pass** (visible beyond the roller at 0–6 s), so it was rolled earlier (inference). It's the best visual evidence in the clip of a closed, rolled surface, but it isn't this pass's work.
- **Milky grey film and slurry (12.5–17 s):** a light grey, milky or foamy film on the wet surface. This could be fines brought up by wet rolling, or the binder solution (inference, can't tell which).
- **Tyre marks:** a clear repeating **diamond tread print** from the rear tyres across the wet film (13–22 s), plus darker exposed patches, which may be thinner film or wet stone (inference).
- **Foot traffic:** dark, boot-print-like marks in the smooth lane (18–22 s) suggest someone walked on it after rolling (inference).
- **Edge detail:** a strip of loose dark gravel between the rolled lane and the concrete column footings, so the edge looks unfinished (17.5–20.5 s; inference).
- **Fall and levels:** not assessable. There are no levels, pegs or stringlines in shot and no ponding pattern that could be read as a fall.
- **Compaction evidence:** only visual (smooth closed surface behind the drum versus the open aggregate elsewhere). No density test, nuclear gauge or CBR sampling appears.

**What the clip proves:** a smooth-drum roller wet-rolls a treated or treatable granular base under a shed, with one crew member spraying liquid ahead of the drum and a second out on the hose line, and an adjacent, earlier-rolled edge lane is smooth and closed.
**What it doesn't prove:** what's in the hose, the dosage, full-depth mixing (C6), the treated depth (~75 mm, C3), the density achieved, bond or strength gain, cure time, or trafficability.
