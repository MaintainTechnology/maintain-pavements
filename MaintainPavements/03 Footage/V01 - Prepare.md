# V01: Track loader with a slatted frame on the loose base, then hand-hose wetting

> [!summary] What this video is about
> A yellow CAT-badged compact track loader (tracked skid-steer) works on the loose, scarified-looking gravel base under a large open-sided steel shed. It carries a slatted steel frame on its grey bucket, first reversing away across the pad and then turning and advancing toward the camera with the frame lowered to base level. It appears to set the frame down and leave it (inference): the frame lies flat and detached on the base at 10.0 s and again at 15.5-16.0 s, and the bucket is empty at 15.0 s. The camera then pans to a worker in orange hi-vis hand-hosing a darker strip of base along the shed edge, and the loader reverses back through the wetted area. The opening wide shot is the source of Jon's own **"01 — Trimming the scarified base"** photo on p.3 of the capability statement, and it's the only clip in the `01-prepare` folder for [[Step 1 - Prepare]]. The clip itself doesn't show a trimming cut conclusively; "trimming" is Jon's label. For the promo it gives a strong portrait establishing shot. It does not show dosing, mixing, grading, rolling or a finished surface.

## Deep analysis

### Process: what this shows about SuperBase

This is **Step 01 Prepare** ([[Step 1 - Prepare]]), and Jon's own document confirms it: the p.3 photo captioned "01 — TRIMMING THE SCARIFIED BASE" is a frame from this clip. Jon's step 01 text reads:

> "Loose stone and bull dust are graded off and the existing yard base is scarified to approximately 75 mm. Additional roadbase is imported only where design levels require it — usually the material already in the yard is all that is needed." ([[SuperBase Capability Statement - Rev A]], p.3)

What the pixels show:
- **Surface state.** A loose, open-textured grey gravel base with no binding or skin. It's covered in deep, overlapping chevron imprints from rubber tracks, and in places (5.5-10.0 s) in parallel furrows. That is consistent with a scarified, not-yet-treated base (inference). No dust plume rises from the base apart from the low haze at the tracks (14.0-16.0 s). Whether the base is damp outside the hose strip can't be told from colour alone. Only the hose strip shows active wetting, and there's no standing water or surface sheen. There's no loose-stone windrow being bladed off in shot, though a raised, darker strip along the shed edge (10.5-13.5 s) may be a windrow or edge berm (inference).
- **Working method.** The loader carries a slatted, ladder-like steel frame on its grey bucket. The frame is carried tilted up (0.0-6.5 s) and lowered flat to base level (7.0-9.5 s) as the machine advances. By 10.0 s it lies flat on the gravel under the bucket lip. At 15.0 s the bucket is empty, and at 15.5-16.0 s the frame lies on the base at the right, clear of the loader. So the visible sequence is **carry, lower and set down**; it may be positioned there for later use (inference). The footage doesn't show the frame being dragged, so "used as a drag or trimming frame" stays an unconfirmed inference. Jon's caption calls the wide shot "trimming"; treat that as his label, not something the pixels demonstrate. The loader reverses across the pad at 0.0-4.5 s and again at 14.0-16.0 s.
- **Moisture.** At 11.0-13.5 s a worker hand-hoses a strip of base. Whether this is plain water (moisture conditioning or dust control during Prepare) or SuperBase dosed with water (step 02) **cannot be determined from the footage**. The mist is colourless.
- **Levels and fall.** Not measurable. A tripod-mounted instrument with a yellow top near the barrier (12.0-14.0 s) could be level control (inference). No pegs, stringlines or level marks are visible on the pad itself. There's **no evidence for [[Step 3 - Grade to Level]]**: no grader, and no treated base being trimmed to crossfall.
- **Compaction.** None. No roller is present and the surface is visibly loose.

**What the clip proves:** a loose gravel base under the shed is being worked by a single tracked machine, and the work area is delineated. That the base is the existing, still-untreated one is an inference, supported by Jon's step 01 caption.
**What it does not prove:** the scarification depth (~75 mm), that no roadbase was imported, any dosing or mixing through the depth, grading to crossfall, compaction, or the finished result.
