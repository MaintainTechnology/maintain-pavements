# V02A: Water cart and hand hose dosing the base under the shed

> [!summary] What this video is about
> A handheld phone clip filmed under a large open-sided steel shelter on a bushland site. It starts beside a row of IBC totes, where one worker hand-sprays the loose, dark, wet gravel base with a hose and a second worker walks the pad. The camera then tracks close along a yellow articulated water cart (tank decal "740"), which is moving with its rear spray heads running (the spray is probably already visible as the mist bank at frame-left from about 5.5 s; inference), and then drives away across the pad, leaving a glossy, saturated surface behind it. This is Jon's **02 Dose and mix** step, and the folder label is correct. The frame at about 24.5 s is almost certainly the source of the "02 — Binder and water through the full depth" photo on page 3 of the capability statement. For the promo it is the strongest "water cart" footage we have (C5), but it shows water going *onto* the surface. It does not show mixing through depth, so text over it has to be chosen carefully (see Cautions).

## Deep analysis

### Process: what this shows about SuperBase

This is **step 02, Dose and mix** ([[Step 2 - Dose and Mix]]). The capability statement says (p.3):

> "SuperBase is introduced with water at a binder-to-water ratio set against your own base material, and mixed through the full depth of the layer."

Jon captioned a still from this clip *"02 — Binder and water through the full depth"*.

**Surface state.** The pad under the shelter is dark grey to black, coarse, loose aggregate: individual stones are visible, with no bound skin. It is saturated. Standing water lies in long puddles along ridges and low lines (0.0 to 8.0 s), and from 19.0 s the pad has a strong specular sheen with a ridged, rippled texture. The ridges could be scarified or ripped texture, or cart tyre tracks (inference; not resolvable). Near the camera (21.0 to 27.0 s) there is a lighter brown-grey, dry, sunlit margin, which gives an obvious wet/dry contrast. **No drum marks** and no smooth compacted surface are visible. Tyre marks exist only as disturbed wet gravel. Fall or crossfall **cannot be judged**: the puddling suggests the pad is not yet trimmed to level at this stage (inference).

**What it proves (visually).** Water, and possibly binder-dosed water (see below), is applied by an articulated water cart with rear spray heads and by a hand hose, onto the existing loose base, under a shelter, with the area bunted off. It supports the "water cart" part of C5. It also fits "the base you already have" (C4): no imported pavement is visible, only a loose gravel base.

**What it does not prove.**
- That the liquid contains SuperBase. The IBCs look cream/milky (contents versus bottle colour not resolvable) and the label is not legible. The link between the IBCs, the hose and the cart is not shown (inference only).
- Any **mixing through depth**. No grader, mixer or rip-and-mix pass appears. The clip shows application onto the surface only.
- Dosage rate, binder-to-water ratio, depth, compaction or CBR.
- Which moisture process or pass this is. Jon says the binder "runs through all moisture processes", so this may be a first dose or a later pass (open question).

> [!warning] Mechanism mismatch risk
> Jon's document says SuperBase's "primary mode is not a surface spray". This clip, taken alone, *looks* like a surface spray. In the edit, pair it with mixing, grading or rolling footage ([[Step 3 - Grade to Level]], [[Step 4 - Roll and Compact]]) and keep "Built, not coated" / "mixed through the full depth" type lines off the frames where only spraying is visible, or put them on the next shot.

The spectrogram shows persistent low bands below about 400 Hz and a steady tonal band around 1.3 to 1.4 kHz from 0 to about 16 s, which fades after that (inference: a pump or engine harmonic).

The centroid is 606 to 858 Hz and spectral flatness is higher (0.53 to 0.62), meaning noisier and broadband.
