# V03B: Low-sun roller pass under the shed (CAT CS56B, fleet no. 5335)

> [!summary] What this video is about
> A yellow CAT single smooth-drum roller (side decals legible as **5335** and **CS56B**) travels across the dark grey pad inside the large open-sided steel shed, in low, warm sunlight. It starts in a rear-quarter view, passes the handheld camera side-on at close range (about 3.5–5.5 s), then recedes engine-first toward the bright open side of the shed, showing its front and smooth drum. The roller moves rear-first, so it's reversing (inference). The soundtrack's pulsed alarm-like signal backs that up. This is Jon's **step 04 Roll and compact**, which matches the folder. It shows a roller pass, but not the "wet" part of wet rolling: no water, sheen or spray is visible. For the promo it's the cleanest, most atmospheric roller clip. The machine is clean, the roof lines lead the eye, there's golden backlight, and the empty floor in the lower half of the frame leaves room for supers. **7.0–10.0 s: Recede into backlight.** The roller moves away rear-first toward the bright open side. Warm flare glows on its rear body and a warm strip of light lies across the floor. The pan slows to almost nothing. It's the most atmospheric stretch, but the highlights in the opening clip.

## Deep analysis

### Process: what this shows about SuperBase

**Step.** This is Jon's step 04, **Roll and compact** ([[Step 4 - Roll and Compact]]). The Capability Statement describes it as wet rolling to final compaction with a smooth drum roller. Its photo caption reads *"04 — Wet rolling to final compaction"*, and the page headline is *"Compaction builds the bond. Testing proves it."* The machine matches exactly: a single **smooth-drum** roller making a pass over the pad. There's a wrinkle, though. Jon's PDF uses a near-identical still of this roller in this light (p3_img3) to illustrate **step 03** (*"Trimmed to level and crossfall"*, [[Step 3 - Grade to Level]]), yet no grader or trimming appears in this clip. The footage is a roller pass, not grading.

**Direction of travel (inference).** The roller moves engine-first throughout. It comes toward camera-left at 0–4.5 s and recedes toward the open side at 6–14 s. Meanwhile, ground features such as the loose-stone patch move right across the frame as the camera pans left. So the roller is **reversing**. A lit white rear lamp at 0.0–0.5 s is consistent with that (reversing lamp or rear work light; inference). The audio also supports it: a pulsed signal on a steady 0.8 s cycle is loudest while the rear faces the camera and fades once the rear points away and the machine moves off. That fits a rear-mounted reversing alarm (inference), though the effects of distance and orientation can't be separated.

**Surface state.**
- *Moisture/sheen:* the pad looks **dry to barely damp and matte**. Even with the bright opening behind the roller (7–14 s), there's no specular reflection, standing water, spray, water-cart activity or hose. Compare [[V02B - Dose and Mix B|V02B]] and PDF p4_img1, which show visible water ahead of the drum. This clip therefore **doesn't show the "wet" part of wet rolling**.
- *Texture:* dark charcoal-grey, fine-grained, tight and uniform, with mild light–dark mottling and lighter, dusty-looking areas in the near foreground (13–14 s). It reads as an already-compacted or finished surface (inference).
- *Loose stone:* two small patches of darker, coarser, loose-looking stone lie on the floor. One sits near the pass line (visible 3.5–6.5 s) and a second shows in the lower frame from 7.0 s. Whether they're loose aggregate, spill or just a coarser bound texture can't be told from the stills.
- *Drum/tyre marks:* there are no visible drum-edge lines, tyre prints, ruts or material pick-up on the drum or tyres. The drum face looks dry and dusty tan-grey.
- *Fall/levels:* the floor looks planar with no ponding or low spots. Crossfall can't be judged from a handheld wide view.
- *Compaction evidence:* only that a roller is passing over the pad. There's no density testing, nuclear gauge, survey or sampling on screen. Whether the drum was vibrating can't be told from the pixels; the audio's 54 Hz low-frequency peak can't be pinned to engine or drum (see Audio).

**What the clip proves:** a clean, well-kept CAT smooth-drum roller operating on a dark, even pad inside the covered work area, with a delineated perimeter, in low sun. The drum passes without visible rutting or shoving of the surface.

**What it doesn't prove:** that SuperBase is in this layer, that the pass is wet, final or vibratory, any density or CBR outcome, grading to level, or which day in the sequence it was filmed.

### Audio

The pulses after about 11 s are therefore close to the background and only faintly traceable in the spectrogram. That fits a rear-mounted "squawker"-type **reversing alarm** facing the camera at first and pointing away after the pass (inference).
